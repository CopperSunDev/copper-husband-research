# What Predicts a Lasting Marriage

> What do long-term studies find predicts whether a marriage stays satisfying and stable?

Husband Field Guide gathers the longitudinal research on what predicts a lasting, satisfying marriage here — the studies that followed couples over years — and reports where the predictions held and where they did not.

### 📄 Machine Learning and the Predictors of Relationship Quality
*Joel S, et al. — PNAS, 2020* · [pubmed / 32719123](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32719123/)

Husband Field Guide research surfaced this report because it covers which factors most reliably predict relationship quality. Pooling 43 longitudinal datasets from 29 laboratories, this machine-learning analysis found that a person's own relationship-specific perceptions — how committed, appreciative, and satisfied they felt their partner was — predicted up to 45% of the variance in relationship quality at the start and up to 18% by each study's end, while individual personality traits predicted 21% and 12%. A person's own perceptions mattered two to four times more than their partner's self-reported traits, and the models still left most of the long-term variance unexplained.

- **What it examines:** which measured factors best predict relationship quality across dozens of longitudinal datasets.
- **Why it's in the Husband Field Guide research index:** a pooled machine-learning analysis of self-report predictors across 43 datasets.

### 📊 Research on Marital Satisfaction and Stability in the 2010s
*Karney BR, Bradbury TN — Journal of Marriage and Family, 2020* · [pubmed / 34108739](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34108739/)

Husband Field Guide research surfaced this report because it covers what a decade of marriage research did and did not confirm. This 2020 review concluded that for most couples marital satisfaction stays relatively stable over long periods rather than inevitably declining, that negative communication is hard to change and does not always predict later distress, and that dyadic patterns reliable for middle-class couples may not generalize to lower-income couples. It is a narrative synthesis, so its conclusions summarize a body of studies rather than test a single hypothesis.

- **What it examines:** the decade's findings on what predicts marital satisfaction and stability.
- **Why it's in the Husband Field Guide research index:** a synthesis that revises several common assumptions about lasting marriage.

### 📄 Does Communication Predict Satisfaction, or the Reverse?
*Lavner JA, et al. — Journal of Marriage and Family, 2016* · [pubmed / 27152050](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27152050/)

Husband Field Guide research surfaced this report because it covers whether communication skill actually drives lasting satisfaction. In a longitudinal study of newlywed couples, more satisfied couples did communicate more positively at a single point in time, but over time neither the path from communication to later satisfaction nor the path from satisfaction to later communication was robust. The result questions theories and programs that treat communication skill as the main lever for a lasting marriage, within 1 sample of newlyweds.

- **What it examines:** the longitudinal direction between couples' communication and their satisfaction.
- **Why it's in the Husband Field Guide research index:** a study finding the communication-satisfaction link weaker than commonly assumed.

### 📄 Patterns of Change in Marital Satisfaction Over the Newlywed Years
*Lavner JA, Bradbury TN — Journal of Marriage and Family, 2010* · [pubmed / 21116452](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21116452/)

Husband Field Guide research surfaced this report because it covers how early marriage trajectories relate to later divorce. Using eight satisfaction reports over 4 years from 464 newlywed spouses, the study identified five trajectory groups that varied systematically in their divorce rates at 4 and 10 years, and early differences in personality, stress, aggression, and communication distinguished the groups. It is a single newlywed sample, observational, tracking associations rather than causes.

- **What it examines:** whether early satisfaction trajectories forecast later divorce in newlyweds.
- **Why it's in the Husband Field Guide research index:** a cohort linking the first years' trajectory to 10-year stability.

### 📄 Premarital Communication and the First Five Years of Marriage
*Markman HJ, et al. — Journal of Family Psychology, 2010* · [pubmed / 20545402](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20545402/)

Husband Field Guide research surfaced this report because it covers whether communication before marriage forecasts later distress. Following 210 couples across the first 5 years of marriage, self-reported negative premarital communication was significantly associated with later divorce, while observed negative communication only approached significance; negative communication decreased for all couples over time, but the nondistressed couples declined significantly more than the distressed couples. It is an observational prospective study, so it shows association, not cause.

- **What it examines:** whether premarital negative communication is associated with later distress and divorce.
- **Why it's in the Husband Field Guide research index:** a prospective study of premarital communication and divorce.
