# Gratitude and Appreciation

> What does research find about gratitude and appreciation between partners?

Husband Field Guide gathers the research on gratitude and appreciation here — feeling and expressing appreciation for a partner — and reports the associations the studies found, including where appreciation can carry costs.

## Sources (5)

### 📄 Gratitude and Successful Partner Change
*Sisson NM, et al. — Personality and Social Psychology Bulletin, 2025* · [pubmed / 38682755](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38682755/)

Husband Field Guide research surfaced this report because it covers how appreciation relates to a partner's efforts to change. Across an observational sample of 111 couples, a preregistered longitudinal sample of 150 couples, and two experiments of 431 and 725 people, one partner's expressed gratitude for the other's change efforts was linked to greater reported success at making the change, consistently explained by the changing partner's more self-determined motivation. The experiments support a causal reading, though the change was self-reported.

- **What it examines:** whether a partner's expressed gratitude relates to successful change efforts.
- **Why it's in the Husband Field Guide research index:** a multi-method set linking gratitude to autonomous motivation and change.

### 📄 Perceived vs. Expressed Gratitude as a Buffer
*Barton AW, et al. — Journal of Social and Personal Relationships, 2023* · [pubmed / 38911018](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38911018/)

Husband Field Guide research surfaced this report because it covers whether feeling appreciated or expressing appreciation matters more. In 316 African American couples followed across three waves over about 16 months, higher perceived gratitude — feeling appreciated by one's partner — buffered the link between relationship stressors and worse relationship quality, whereas expressed gratitude did not, and the longitudinal protection held only for ineffective arguing, not financial strain. It is observational in one specific population, so it may not generalize.

- **What it examines:** whether perceived or expressed gratitude buffers relationship stressors.
- **Why it's in the Husband Field Guide research index:** a study separating felt appreciation from expressed appreciation.

### 📄 The Hidden Costs of Overstated Gratitude
*Shimshock CJ, et al. — Personality and Social Psychology Bulletin, 2026* · [pubmed / 41342869](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41342869/)

Husband Field Guide research surfaced this report because it covers when gratitude can backfire. Across four dyadic studies of 639 couples — lab conversations, daily life, and over time — expressing or perceiving more gratitude than was genuinely felt, which the authors call 'gratitude amplification,' was linked to personal and relationship costs, explained by lower authenticity and responsiveness. The work suggests appreciation beyond what is felt carries downsides, within correlational and short-term designs.

- **What it examines:** whether expressing more gratitude than is felt carries relationship costs.
- **Why it's in the Husband Field Guide research index:** a study documenting downsides of overstated gratitude.

---

### 📄 How Honesty Shapes the Benefits of Gratitude
*Le BM, et al. — Emotion, 2026* · [pubmed / 41701191](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41701191/)

Husband Field Guide research surfaced this report because it covers whether sincerity changes what gratitude does. In 214 romantic couples studied through daily reports, a lab session, and a 3-month follow-up, both expressing and perceiving sincerity about gratitude predicted greater personal and relationship well-being, while accurately reading a partner's sincerity did not — what a person perceived mattered more than the underlying reality. The findings are correlational and self-reported.

- **What it examines:** whether sincerity about gratitude changes its personal and relationship benefits.
- **Why it's in the Husband Field Guide research index:** a study on the role of sincerity in whether gratitude helps.

### 📄 Expressing Gratitude Increases Time Together: A Field Experiment
*Chang YP, et al. — Scientific Reports, 2022* · [pubmed / 35810173](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35810173/)

Husband Field Guide research surfaced this report because it covers whether deliberately expressing gratitude changes daily behavior. In a 5-week randomized field experiment with daily couple reports, partners randomly assigned to a gratitude-expression plan spent more time physically together in everyday life than a control group, an effect explained by the increase in expressed gratitude. As a randomized experiment it supports a causal reading, though it measured time together rather than relationship quality directly.

- **What it examines:** whether a gratitude-expression intervention increases couples' time together.
- **Why it's in the Husband Field Guide research index:** a randomized field experiment on expressing gratitude.
