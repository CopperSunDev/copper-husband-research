# Fairness and the Division of Labor

> What does research find about the division of labor, fairness, and relationship satisfaction?

Husband Field Guide gathers the research on fairness and the division of household and family labor here — who does what, the mental load, and perceptions of fairness — and reports how they relate to satisfaction, including where the findings conflict.

## Sources (5)

### 📄 The Cognitive Dimension of Household Labor: A Systematic Review
*Reich-Stiebert N, et al. — Sex Roles, 2023* · [pubmed / 37283733](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37283733/)

Husband Field Guide research surfaced this report because it covers the 'mental load' — the planning and remembering behind running a household. This systematic review of 31 studies found that women perform the larger share of cognitive and mental household and childcare labor, especially parenting decisions, and report more related consequences including stress and lower life and relationship satisfaction. As a review of mostly observational studies, it describes patterns rather than establishing cause.

- **What it examines:** who carries the cognitive, mental load of household labor and its reported consequences.
- **Why it's in the Husband Field Guide research index:** a systematic review of the mental-load research.

---

### 📄 Gendered Perceptions of Fairness in Housework and Expenses
*Gillespie BJ, et al. — PLoS One, 2019* · [pubmed / 30893363](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30893363/)

Husband Field Guide research surfaced this report because it covers how perceptions of fairness relate to satisfaction. Among 10,236 survey respondents, feeling that the division was unfair to oneself predicted relationship satisfaction and sexual frequency more strongly than feeling it was unfair to one's partner, and fairness over shared expenses was a stronger predictor of relationship quality than perceived fairness in housework. It is a cross-sectional online survey, so it captures associations at a single point in time.

- **What it examines:** which fairness perceptions predict relationship satisfaction and sexual frequency.
- **Why it's in the Husband Field Guide research index:** a large survey on perceived fairness and relationship quality.

---

### 📄 Household Labor and Sexual Desire in Partnered Mothers
*Harris EA, et al. — Archives of Sexual Behavior, 2022* · [pubmed / 36112330](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36112330/)

Husband Field Guide research surfaced this report because it covers how an unequal division of labor relates to desire. In two samples of partnered mothers (677 and 396 women), doing a larger share of household labor was associated with lower sexual desire for the partner, a link explained by perceiving the partner as dependent and by seeing the division as unfair. Both samples are correlational and limited to mothers partnered with men, so the findings may not generalize.

- **What it examines:** whether an unequal household-labor share relates to lower sexual desire.
- **Why it's in the Husband Field Guide research index:** a study linking labor division to desire through perceived unfairness.

---

### 📄 Dividing Childcare Across the Transition to Parenthood
*Fillo J, et al. — Journal of Personality and Social Psychology, 2015* · [pubmed / 25603377](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25603377/)

Husband Field Guide research surfaced this report because it covers whether splitting childcare evenly predicts satisfaction. Following first-time parents from about 6 weeks before birth to 24 months after, the share of childcare a partner did was not on its own tied to relationship satisfaction; instead, individual differences such as attachment avoidance and perceptions such as childcare confidence and work-family conflict shaped whether a given division predicted satisfaction. It is a longitudinal but observational study.

- **What it examines:** whether the proportion of childcare predicts satisfaction across new parenthood.
- **Why it's in the Husband Field Guide research index:** a study where the childcare split alone did not predict satisfaction.

---

### 📄 Egalitarianism, Housework, and Sexual Frequency
*Kornrich S, et al. — American Sociological Review, 2013* · [pubmed / 25540459](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25540459/)

Husband Field Guide research surfaced this report because it covers a counterintuitive finding about sharing housework. Using National Survey of Families and Households data collected in the 1990s, couples with a more traditional, gender-typical division of housework reported higher sexual frequency, and men doing more of the traditionally female 'core' chores was associated with less frequent sex — contrary to the idea that sharing housework boosts intimacy. The data are observational and decades old, so they may not reflect couples today.

- **What it examines:** the association between housework division and sexual frequency.
- **Why it's in the Husband Field Guide research index:** an older study with a finding that runs counter to the fairness literature.
