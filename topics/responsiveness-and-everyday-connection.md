### 📊 Responsiveness in Romantic Partners' Interactions
*Arican-Dinc B, Gable SL — Current Opinion in Psychology, 2023* · [pubmed / 37515977](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37515977/)

Husband Field Guide research surfaced this report because it covers what researchers mean by 'responsiveness.' This 2023 review defines perceived partner responsiveness as a person's belief that their partner understands, validates, and cares about them, and positions it as a common thread running through support, gratitude, and shared-good-news interactions, with attentive listening as the first step. As a review it synthesizes earlier studies and offers a framework rather than reporting a single new result.

- **What it examines:** the concept of perceived partner responsiveness and the research around it.
- **Why it's in the Husband Field Guide research index:** a review defining responsiveness and its role across interactions.

### 📄 Perceived Responsiveness Links Daily Sexual and Marital Satisfaction
*Gadassi R, et al. — Archives of Sexual Behavior, 2016* · [pubmed / 25680818](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25680818/)

Husband Field Guide research surfaced this report because it covers how feeling understood connects to satisfaction day to day. In 34 newlywed couples who kept daily diaries over 30 days, perceived partner responsiveness partly accounted for the link between a day's sexual satisfaction and marital satisfaction at both the between-person and daily levels; at the daily level the pattern was stronger for women, while no gender difference appeared at the between-person level. The sample is small and correlational, so it shows association rather than cause.

- **What it examines:** whether perceived responsiveness links daily sexual and marital satisfaction.
- **Why it's in the Husband Field Guide research index:** a daily-diary study of responsiveness as a connective thread.

### 📄 Responses to a Partner's Good News Don't Always Align
*Bosisio M, et al. — Journal of Social and Personal Relationships, 2022* · [pubmed / 35872974](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35872974/)

Husband Field Guide research surfaced this report because it covers how partners respond when the other shares good news. In 151 cohabiting couples filmed sharing positive events, self-reported and partner-perceived enthusiastic (active-constructive) responses were linked to the responder's own greater sexual satisfaction, perceived 'quiet but interested' responses were linked to lower satisfaction, and observed responses that undermined the good news were linked to lower sexual function — different ways of measuring the response related to different outcomes. It is a correlational lab study of 151 cohabiting couples.

- **What it examines:** how self-reported, perceived, and observed responses to good news relate to well-being.
- **Why it's in the Husband Field Guide research index:** a study of how responses to a partner's good news relate to well-being.
