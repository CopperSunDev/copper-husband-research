### 📄 A Two-Dimensional Meta-Analysis of Relationship Conflict
*Woodin EM — Journal of Family Psychology, 2011* · [pubmed / 21553964](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21553964/)

Husband Field Guide research surfaced this report because it covers how different conflict behaviors relate to satisfaction across many studies. This meta-analysis of 64 studies and 5,071 couples mapped conflict behavior onto two dimensions and found hostility was robustly associated with lower relationship satisfaction at a medium effect size, while problem-solving and intimacy were each closely associated with relationship satisfaction, also at medium effects, and gender differences were small. It pools correlational studies, so the associations do not establish direction or cause.

- **What it examines:** how hostility, problem-solving, and intimacy during conflict relate to satisfaction.
- **Why it's in the Husband Field Guide research index:** a meta-analysis quantifying conflict behaviors against satisfaction.

### 📄 When Demand-Withdraw Helps: Socioeconomic Context
*Ross JM, et al. — Journal of Personality and Social Psychology, 2019* · [pubmed / 30321045](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30321045/)

Husband Field Guide research surfaced this report because it covers whether 'bad' conflict patterns are universally bad. Across two samples — 515 couples over 18 months and 431 couples assessed across follow-ups to 27 months — the demand-withdraw pattern was linked to worse outcomes for higher-resource couples, but withdrawal was linked to more stable wives' satisfaction, protecting against decline, when socioeconomic risk was high. The association depended on couples' circumstances rather than being fixed; both samples are observational.

- **What it examines:** whether the effect of demand-withdraw depends on socioeconomic context.
- **Why it's in the Husband Field Guide research index:** a study where a classic 'toxic' pattern reversed by context.

### 📄 When Negative Behaviors Are Positive
*McNulty JK, Russell VM — Journal of Personality and Social Psychology, 2010* · [pubmed / 20307131](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20307131/)

Husband Field Guide research surfaced this report because it covers when directness during conflict helps versus harms. In 2 longitudinal newlywed studies, direct negative problem-solving — blame, commands, rejection — predicted steeper satisfaction declines when problems were minor but more stable satisfaction when problems were severe, an effect mediated by changes in the problems' severity; indirect negativity was associated with stably lower satisfaction regardless of problem severity. The studies are observational samples of newlyweds.

- **What it examines:** whether direct negativity helps or harms depending on problem severity.
- **Why it's in the Husband Field Guide research index:** a study where blunt negativity tracked better outcomes for severe problems.

### 📄 When Poor Communication Does and Does Not Matter
*Nguyen TP, et al. — Journal of Family Psychology, 2020* · [pubmed / 32077736](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32077736/)

Husband Field Guide research surfaced this report because it covers whether communication quality matters equally for all couples. In 414 newlywed couples observed four times at 9-month intervals, rising negativity coincided with lower satisfaction for husbands only when they were under greater stress than usual, and weaker problem-solving was associated with lower satisfaction only among spouses with chronically high cumulative stress. The findings come from newlyweds and are concurrent associations, so they may not generalize or establish cause.

- **What it examines:** whether stress moderates when communication quality affects satisfaction.
- **Why it's in the Husband Field Guide research index:** a study where communication mattered mainly under high stress.
